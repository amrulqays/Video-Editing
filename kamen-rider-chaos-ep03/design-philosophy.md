# Sumi Impact

**Philosophy:** Collision rendered as ink. Two opposing bodies meet along a single diagonal, and the exact point where they touch is the only white on the page. Everything else is smoke, pigment and armour, and all of it falls toward that point.

**Space and form.** The page is cut by one diagonal running from the upper-left corner to the lower-right. Mass sits at the two ends of it and the centre stays empty, so the eye is pulled into the gap. Bodies are cropped hard and dissolve at their edges into wash and splatter. Nothing stands on the ground and nothing is shown whole: forms come out of darkness only as far as the collision needs them. The negative space is fog with weight, with no flat fills anywhere.

**Colour and material.** The base is a cold monochrome of graphite, ash, wet slate and lamp-black ink. Saturated colour is rationed to two signals, one opposing hue per side, and each appears only where it would glow: eyes, cores and the energy leaking from a wound in the air. At the centre the two hues burn out to pure white. Surfaces stay physical: brushed metal catches a rim of the impact light, ink pools and spatters with real droplets and satellite specks, and film grain sits over everything so the image reads as photographed rather than drawn. Every gradient should look hand-balanced, as if the grading took days.

**Scale and rhythm.** Splatter repeats at three scales (pooled mass, flung droplet, airborne mist). Sparks and shards repeat in the same way, dense at the centre and thinning toward the edges, like a measured explosion. Lettering follows the same rhythm: one monumental mark, a few mid-weight lines and a whisper of fine print, with long quiet intervals between them.

**Typography as gesture.** There is very little text, and every piece of it is placed with intent. Vertical columns of serif kanji stand at the margins like inscriptions on a blade. One mark (the title) is built by hand from sharp strokes and treated as a physical object with its own light. Fine print is tracked wide and kept small. No line touches another element, and every margin has been measured and measured again.

**Craft.** The work should look like it took weeks of compositing by someone at the top of the field: each edge feathered by hand, each glow balanced against the grain, each droplet placed where gravity would send it. If something feels unresolved, the answer is never another element. The answer is more care given to the ones already there.
